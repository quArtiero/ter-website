Join the team · 2026–27

No experience necessary.

Open to every Tufts undergraduate and graduate student, any major. You will learn CAD, machining, circuits, PCBs, budgets and outreach from people who learned it here a year ago.

How it works

Three steps, no application

  1. 01

    Show up on a Sunday

    All teams meet Sundays at 12 pm in the Garage at Bray Labs, 504 Boston Ave. Come to any one — there is nothing to sign up for first.

  2. 02

    Pick a project team

    Each project team meets once during the week; times are set by When2Meet once teams form. Sit in on a few before you choose — switching later is normal.

  3. 03

    Build an RC car first

    Optional for new members: a ten-week program in Nolop — CAD, motors, batteries, ESCs and control loops — ending in practice laps. Tuesdays at 7 pm or Wednesdays at noon.

Weekly schedule

  • Sun · 12 pm All-team meeting — Garage at Bray Labs. Everyone, every week.
  • Weekdays Project team meetings — one per team, set each semester. [TIMES PER TEAM]
  • Tue 7 pm · Wed 12 pm New Member RC program — Nolop makerspace. Pick either slot.

Announcements, room changes and the schedule live in Slack.

What you will learn

Electrical

  • SPICE and PCB development
  • High-voltage systems and motor drives
  • Embedded code and lab testing

Mechanical

  • CAD and CAM in industry software
  • Welding, machining, 3D printing
  • Composites, materials and R&D

Business

  • Project goals and budgets
  • Fundraising, outreach, networking
  • Communication and graphic design

Hands-on automotive experience for everyone — and you will be years ahead of your classes.

Project teams

Where you could end up

Every team below is working on TER27, the new car. Most members sit on one project team; many wander between two.

Electrical

Low Voltage
Wiring harness and safety systems, power conversion, indicators and communication.
Accumulator / High Voltage
An all-new accumulator: battery cells plus the components that feed the drivetrain.
BMS
A custom battery management system that balances and monitors the pack.
Sensors
Hardware and software to collect and use vehicle data.

Mechanical

Chassis
The first frame built from scratch since 2019.
Suspension & Brakes
Force analysis, control-arm jigs, steering, and a pedal box that stops the car without fighting the driver.
Drivetrain
Mounting the motor and the differential — how the car moves.
Cooling & Aero · Seat
CFD for drag and downforce, molds by 3D printing and CNC, composite parts; a seat in sheet metal or carbon fiber.
Accumulator enclosure & cart
The high-voltage enclosure and the steel cart that carries it, built around the safety rules.

Business Operations

Sponsorships & Outreach
Sponsor relations, the handbook and resume book, the alumni newsletter.
Budget & Purchasing
The annual budget, purchasing, and the paperwork with Tufts offices for big buys.
Social Media & Events
Instagram and this website, merch and posters, the TER27 livery, team events.
Logistics & Compliance
Getting the team to New Hampshire, and keeping car, equipment and workspace to Tufts and FSAE standards.
Questions

Things people ask at the club fair

Do you actually build a car?
Yes.
I have absolutely no experience. Is that a problem?
No. Most of us started here with none. We teach CAD, circuits and fabrication from zero, and the RC car program exists for exactly this.
Can non-engineers join?
Yes. Business Operations runs sponsorships, budget, media and logistics, and there is art and design work — merch, posters, the car's livery.
Who drives the car at competition?
Usually the most senior, most committed members.
How many zip ties are on the car?
A lot.
Are you the coolest club on campus?
Yes.
