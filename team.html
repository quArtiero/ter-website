Meet the team · 2026–27

Ninety students, one garage.

Undergraduates and graduate students from the School of Engineering and the School of Arts and Sciences — mechanical and electrical engineers, computer scientists, and the business and visual-arts people who keep the lights on.

Chiefs

Three chiefs lead the team — one each for operations, mechanical and electrical — with a board of project leads under each.

  • Quinn Karm

    Chief Operations Officer · BA'27

    Runs Business Operations: sponsorships, budget, media and the logistics of getting the team to New Hampshire.

  • B Epstein-Reyes

    Chief Mechanical Engineer · E'28

    Leads the mechanical board: chassis, suspension, brakes, drivetrain, aero and the accumulator enclosure for TER27.

  • Taiyr Ashkenov

    Chief Electrical Engineer · E'28

    Leads the electrical board: accumulator and high voltage, the custom BMS, low-voltage harness, sensors and data.

Since 2008

Our story

  1. 2008

    Founding

    Started by Matt Liberatore (E'09) with the School of Engineering Dean's Office and the Peter and Denise Wittich Family Fund. First entry places 2nd in the Hybrid-in-Progress category.

  2. 2010

    Hybrid competition

    An engine joins the electric drivetrain. Tufts Hybrid Racing finishes 12th of 19 hybrids.

  3. 2015

    All-electric, first place

    The only all-electric entry to get on track wins the Electric Division. The team becomes Tufts Electric Racing.

  4. 2019

    Back on track

    The high-voltage system is fully integrated and the car races again, finishing 5th.

  5. 2023

    Comeback

    After losing two graduating classes to COVID, First Charge returns to New Hampshire for the static events — and the team grows from a handful to ~90.

  6. 2027

    TER27

    The first chassis built from scratch since 2019 is set to race at Formula Hybrid+Electric 2027.
